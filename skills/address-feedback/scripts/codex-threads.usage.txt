Usage: codex-threads.sh OWNER/REPO PR [SINCE_COMMENT_ID]

SINCE_COMMENT_ID limits the inline findings to comment ids greater than it,
which is how you read only what arrived after the last round.

Inline findings print as:

  === <comment id> <path>:<line> ===
  <body>

Feed those comment ids to reply-and-resolve.sh.

A root that carries a reply of yours while its thread still reads unresolved
prints as:

  === <comment id> <path>:<line> answered-unresolved ===
  <the finding, tidied as above>
  --- your reply <reply id> ---
  <that reply, tidied the same way>
  <one line saying to check the reply before resolving>

The newest reply of yours under the root is the one shown - the answer a reader
reaches last. Read it: a reply is not a disposition because it exists, and an
earlier "Can you clarify?" leaves the finding unanswered. Where the text you hand
reply-and-resolve.sh matches any reply of yours under that root, it posts nothing
and resolves the thread; any other text is posted as a new reply before the
resolve.

SINCE_COMMENT_ID never hides these: the reply that needs resolving was posted in
an earlier round, so its id is below any cutoff you would pass.

A root the reviewer took back while its thread is still open prints as:

  === <comment id> <path>:<line> withdrawn-open ===
  <the finding, tidied as above>
  <one line saying to resolve it without answering>

Whether a thread reads resolved and whether the reviewer withdrew the finding are
two different questions, so the withdrawal is read the same way here as on a
resolved thread: the newest comment under the root by anyone but you, by the
shape below.
Such a root owes no answer - the reviewer took the finding back - and it does owe
the resolve that closes the thread. Give its comment id to reply-and-resolve.sh
with `--resolve-withdrawn`, which checks the withdrawal again through the API and
resolves the thread without posting anything. No SINCE_COMMENT_ID filter: the
withdrawal came in a later round than the finding.

Review bodies print as:

  === top-level-response review <review id> <state> <sha7> (<head>) ===
  <body>

A review body has no thread and no comment id: answer it with a top-level PR
comment. reply-and-resolve.sh takes review-thread roots only and refuses a
review id. Bodies on a superseded head are listed too - a superseded anchor does
not retire a finding - and the list is not narrowed by round, so check your own
earlier top-level responses before answering one again. Codex's boilerplate
"Codex Review" container is the one body left out; it carries no finding. Every
other body is listed with its review state, so an APPROVED sign-off is
distinguishable from a COMMENTED finding without dropping either.

Two substitutions run on each body, and nothing else changes: a priority badge
image is reduced to [P1]/[P2]/[P3], and Codex's footer line is dropped. A badge
is an image whose alt text starts with the priority, or one served from
img.shields.io; any other image, including a screenshot a reviewer attached, is
left as it stands. The footer is the exact line `Useful? React with 👍 / 👎.`,
with or without the full stop and with any blanks around it, and only a line that
carries the footer and nothing else is dropped. A line that opens with that
phrase and carries on, and a sentence that mentions it mid-line, are finding text
and stay whole.

A root whose thread reads resolved while no reply of yours is under it prints as:

  === <comment id> <path>:<line> resolved-unanswered ===
  <the finding, tidied as above>
  <one line saying the thread is closed and the finding is not>

Resolved is not answered. Somebody - a maintainer, an automation - closed the
thread without your disposition, and the finding still owes one. Give its comment
id to reply-and-resolve.sh with the answer: that script posts onto a resolved
thread and leaves it resolved. SINCE_COMMENT_ID never hides these either: the
resolve happened in a later round than the finding.

A root whose thread reads resolved while a reply of yours is under it prints as:

  === <comment id> <path>:<line> resolved-replied ===
  <the finding, tidied as above>
  --- your reply <reply id> ---
  <the newest reply of yours under that root, tidied the same way>
  --- <author> replied again <comment id> ---          (only where there is one)
  <that comment, tidied the same way>
  <one line saying to check that reply before treating the root as done>

A closed thread does not turn a reply into a disposition, so this section is the
root no reply of yours answers. The disposition format is the skill's own, in
../SKILL.md: a reply that starts with `Fixed in ` and a commit id of 7 to 40 hex
characters, or with `Won't fix:`, both case-sensitive and both read after the two
substitutions above and with leading whitespace removed. A root carrying no such
reply - an earlier "Can you clarify?", a note to yourself, a reply that says a fix
is coming - prints here: read it against the finding, and where it is not the
answer give the root's comment id to reply-and-resolve.sh, which posts onto the
resolved thread and leaves it resolved.

A disposition answers the finding only while it is the last word on the thread.
Where anyone but you posted under that root after your newest disposition - the
bot saying the fix misses a path, a maintainer saying the finding still stands -
that comment is newer, the finding is open again, and the root prints here with
the comment and its author shown under your reply. Answer it with a new
disposition. A root whose newest disposition has nothing from another author
after it is finished work and prints in no section, whatever else you posted
after it. No SINCE_COMMENT_ID filter here either.

The one root no section asks you to answer is one the reviewer withdrew.
That is the newest comment under the root by anyone but you, provided it is by
the same bot and its whole body, after
the two substitutions above and with leading and trailing whitespace removed, is
one line carrying the withdrawal and nothing else: `Withdrawn`, `Retracted`,
`This finding no longer applies`, `Outdated` or `Outdated: this finding no longer
applies`, each with an optional full stop, all in any capitalisation. `Withdrawn.`
and `Outdated: this finding no longer applies.` are withdrawals. Any other
wording keeps the root listed: a sentence that opens with a marker and carries
on, such as `Withdrawn funds can bypass the balance guard.` or `Outdated: cache
entries can bypass tenant scoping`, a marker inside a longer sentence, a body
that runs to a second line, and a negation such as `This finding has not been
withdrawn; it still applies.`

Only that newest comment decides. Where a withdrawal is followed by anything else
from anyone but you - the bot saying the finding still applies, a maintainer
saying it still fails on the async path, another reviewer restating it - the root
is not withdrawn and prints as any other root does. A comment of your own after
the withdrawal changes nothing, so your "ok" under it leaves the root withdrawn.
A root listed in error costs a reply nobody needed; a root dropped in error is a
finding that disappears.

A withdrawn root that reads resolved is finished and prints in no section. A
withdrawn root whose thread is still open prints in the `withdrawn-open` section
above, which is the one thing left to do with it.

Every read must come back with a body. A `gh api` response of zero bytes, or one
that is not JSON, is an unread response even where `gh` exited 0 - GitHub writes
an empty collection as `[]` - so the run exits 1 and names the read. An empty
inventory therefore means nothing needs an answer; it never means a feed went
unread. Both feeds are read before anything prints, so a failed read leaves no
partial inventory behind.
